# PHP Community Feedback Summary — April 2026

## About these Findings

During the first few weeks of my tenure as The PHP Foundation’s Executive Director, I wanted to hear what was on the minds of the PHP community, so I embarked on a sort of “Listening Tour.” These slides represent some of the key themes that floated to the top.

Elizabeth Barron — April 2026


**PEOPLE LOVE PHP**

This feedback presents many opportunities for us to improve the ecosystem as a whole, but you should know that people still LOVE PHP.

**SMALL SAMPLE BUT REPRESENTATIVE**

I spoke with around 60 people from 18 countries, and from all parts of the PHP ecosystem. Your experience may not resonate, and that’s ok. PHP is a big community.

**THESE FINDINGS REPRESENT OPINIONS**

To be clear, these findings are not indisputable facts, but they represent input from the community. They do not represent any official opinion of The PHP Foundation.

**STILL LOOKING FOR FEEDBACK**

This is not the end! My virtual door is always open to listen to your thoughts, ideas, and concerns.

## The PHP Foundation: Operations and Overall Effectiveness

There is a perceived lack of clarity, coordination, and structure

* **Priorities of Work**
  
  The processes for prioritization and decision making are unclear to the community.

* **Working in Siloes**

  There could be better coordination between contractors.

* **Responsiveness**

  Shared work among contractors would help improve responsiveness to issues and PRs.

* **Unclear Work Assignments**

  It is unclear to the community who is working on what.

_“Priorities seem random”_

_“People are doing their own thing”_

_“No one is coordinating which RFCs fit together”_

_“There is no Foundation Roadmap”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

---

## The PHP Foundation: Communication and Transparency

Lack of visibility is eroding trust with the community

* **Ambiguity of Foundation Role**
  
  The relationship between the Foundation, the PHP Language, and the PHP Community is unclear.

* **Missing Clarity About Plans**
  
  The Foundation communicates the “what” but not the “why,” and is not forward-looking.

* **Irregular Communication Cadence**
  
  A more consistent communication cadence would improve transparency and build trust with the community.

* **Decisions are Shared After the Fact**
  
  Communications feel reactive and could happen sooner in the process.

_“No one knows what the Foundation is doing”_

_“People know the Foundation exists—that’s it”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## The PHP Foundation: Fundraising and Sponsorship

Funding is critical but could use support and coordination

* **Ambiguity of Fundraising Strategy**

   It is not clear when or if proactive fundraising is happening.

* **Stronger Sponsorship Relationships are Needed**

  There is not a great feedback loop or consistent sponsor engagement.

* **Fundraising is Ever More Challenging**

  A changing fundraising landscape is making it more difficult to raise funds across the open source ecosystem.

* **Unclear Value Proposition for Sponsors**

  Clarity around benefits to sponsors and more information about what sponsors actually need would be helpful.

_“Fundraising is the most important priority”_

_“We don’t know what matters to sponsors”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## PHP Marketing and Outreach: Marketing, Branding, & the Perception of PHP

There is a gap between reality and perception

* **PHP can be Perceived as Outdated and Crusty**

  There is a perception outside the PHP bubble which might cause people to overlook PHP as a viable option.

* **The Perception Does Not Match Today’s PHP**

  The association people make with PHP is based on old assumptions about the language. People are not aware of the modernized PHP.

* **Competing Languages Have More Visibility**

  Other open source languages are talked about and more visible in the open source ecosystem.

* **No Coordinated Storytelling**

  There is no coordinated community effort in telling the story of PHP’s evolution or highlighting real world usage.

_“Everything people mocked us for is gone”_

_“People think PHP stopped at 5.4”_

_“No one is talking about PHP; they are talking about Python or JavaScript”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

---

## PHP Marketing and Outreach: Advocacy, and Representation

PHP lacks a voice outside the ecosystem

* **Limited PHP Presence at Open Source Events**

  PHP talks either aren’t being submitted or they aren’t being accepted at open source conferences as often as they could be.

* **No Coordinated Advocacy Function**

  People advocating for PHP are one-offs and not part of a unified effort.

* **Imbalanced Representation**

  PHP does not have enough representation in broader open source initiatives and policy discussions.

* **Lack of Engagement with Decision Makers**

  Decision makers inside companies do not have accurate information about current day PHP.

_“Nobody is talking about PHP”_

_“We are not present where decisions are made”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: Growing the Community

The PHP community is strong, but fragmented

* **Global Community is Large but Lacks Cohesion**

  There are thousands of PHP developers across the globe but it is difficult to bring them all together.

* **Growth is Slowing and Retention is Declining**

  New users are coming to the language, but people are also switching to other languages.

* **Community Growth is Underresourced**

  It’s difficult to find resources for community leaders and initiatives that drive community growth.

* **Frameworks and Ecosystem Participants Operate in Silos**

   The language would be more powerful with stronger collaboration across ecosystem components.

_“It’s worrying that I recognize so many people at a conference.”_

_“It is crucial for the language to realize the potential of the community and ecosystem.”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: New User Experience

Onboarding in PHP can be difficult and inconsistent

* **Onboarding Experience is Inconsistent**

  The onboarding experience seems to be different for everyone, with many relying on various communities and social channels for help.

* **No Clear Onboarding Path**

  New users have difficulty knowing where to start.

* **The Setup Experience Could be Easier**

  Setup for a newcomer can be tricky and complicated.

* **No Centralized Place to Get Help**

  It’s difficult for a newcomer to find help or know who to ask to find answers.

_“Onboarding is huge. It’s our biggest hurdle with growing the community”_

_“Hard to know where to start”_

_“Newcomers may not know they can come into Discord and ask questions that will be answered by experts”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: Education and Employment

The PHP ecosystem lacks a clear and supported pathway from learning to long-term career growth.

* **PHP Has Limited Presence in Formal Education**

  In many places, students are only taught Python and Javascript. PHP does not consistently show up in higher learning or bootcamps.

* **There is a Fragmented Educational Ecosystem**

  Some students are taught Laravel or Symfony but not PHP.

* **Developers are Leaving PHP for Better Opportunities**

  Even if they want to stay with PHP, some are forced to switch languages because PHP jobs are hard to find.

* **Decentralized Job Matching Makes it More Difficult to Find Relevant PHP Jobs**

  Job postings in the ecosystem are fragmented and spread across various sources making job hunting more challenging.

_“PHP limits my job opportunities as a senior software engineer”_

_“Bootcamps teach React, not PHP”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: Learning Resources

PHP is strong in reference material, but there is a gap for new learners

* **PHP Manual is Strong, but Not Meant for Beginners**

  The PHP.net Manual is an excellent reference but assumes the reader already has a base of knowledge.

* **There is a Learning Gap for Newcomers**

  Many existing tutorials and other resources already assume the user has some knowledge of PHP.

* **Learning Resources are Scattered and Can Be Outdated**

  It is difficult to find verified and relevant tutorials and examples because there are many scattered resources that have not been verified for accuracy.

* **Mentorship is Difficult to Find**

  Mentorship programs that used to exist in PHP have become inactive without sustainable support.

_“People see these outdated resources and shouldn’t be using them to learn.”_

_“The Manual assumes a level of knowledge already”_

_“The Manual is an excellent resource but doesn’t help you learn the language”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: User Groups and Conferences

Community spaces exist but need support

* **User Groups Need Help**

  User groups are still recovering from COVID-era restrictions, but they are fragile and can use support in attracting attendees and finding venue space.

* **Conferences are Facing Sustainability Challenges**

  PHP ecosystem conferences could use help with promotion, funding, finding speakers, and attracting attendees.

* **Diversity and Inclusion Remain as Issues**

  It is still difficult for conference organizers to attract a diverse pool of attendees and speakers.

* **There Could be More Coordination Among User Groups and Conferences**

  User groups and conferences are often working in silos instead of coordinating with each other.

_“User groups are coming back!”_

_“Some conferences are struggling”_

_“There are some things you can solve in person that you can’t online”_

_“I really miss being able to go to my user group meetings”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## Community Growth and New User Pipeline: Ecosystem Sustainability

The PHP Ecosystem is thriving but at risk

* **Bus/Lottery/Contributor Absence Factor Still Exists**

  Many PHP related projects suffer from lack of sustainable maintainer teams.

* **Burnout is Common and There is a Lack of Support**

  Ecosystem maintainers are feeling burned out and there are no support systems in place to help them.

* **Funding is Limited**

  Smaller projects could use financial support.

* **Contributor Pipeline and Retention Could be Stronger**

  There is a lack of new contributors, especially those that stick around long enough to gain the trust of maintainers.

_“Burnout is real”_

_“Some projects have no succession plan”_

_“My project could use money to help me mentor new contributors”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## PHP The Language: RFC Process

The decision making process has worked for many years, but there may be room for improvement

* **Many People Have Difficulty Navigating the Mailing List**

   Those who do have difficulty navigating the list could use more support and the discussions could use more visibility.

* **Ecosystem Representation and Engagement Could Be Improved in the Current Voting System**

  The voting system is not as reflective of active participants and those with a vested interest in the future of the language as it could be.

* **Lack of Code of Conduct Creates Inconsistency in Interactions**

  The experience with interacting on the php-internals mailing list varies widely.

_“I wish that the community would participate more in the discussions of the RFCs they care about.”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## PHP The Language: Contributor Experience

There is still some friction with setup and installation.

* **Some Critical Extensions Could be Added by Default**

  Things like debugging could be more integrated into the main language.

* **Contribution Process can be Slow**

  Long response times on PRs or issues can discourage contributors.

* **Internals Documentation Could be Improved**

  Existing resources aren’t the most up-to-date and document review can be a bottleneck.

* **Setup and Installation can be Tricky**

  More research could be done to better understand the installation experience.

_“PRs take months to merge”_

_“Setup is better than it was, but still hard”_

_“Internals documentation is critical and Internals Book is not fully updated”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## PHP The Language: Overall Vision for the Language

Decision by committee makes it challenging to present a unified vision

* **Not Having a Long Term Vision Makes it Challenging for Others to Plan**

  A long term roadmap would be helpful to users and frameworks.

* **Companies Want to Know What’s Coming**

  Without predictability, companies may choose other languages.

* **PHP’s Challenges are Not Technical, But Perceptual**

  This ties into perception and storytelling challenges.

* **A Long Term Vision Could Ease Tension Between BC and New Features**

  Knowing what will be deprecated helps people prepare ahead of time.

_“People need to know someone is steering the ship”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.

## PHP The Language: AI

This is going to be a difficult thing to agree on, but it is on the minds of many.

* **No Unified Open Source Stance Makes it Challenging**

   There is no widely accepted industry standard.

* **Opportunities Come With Risk**

  Risks include copyright, ownership, code quality, and maintainer burnout.

* **Must Also Consider Ethics and the Environment**

  AI’s impact goes beyond just code.

* **PHP Representation in AI Tooling and Recommendations**

  AI agents are a new audience for PHP content.

_“No agreed vision for AI makes it hard to know what the future will look like”_

_“AI could help newcomers contribute”_

_“AI is here to stay, so we have to figure this out soon”_


Note: these are presented in no particular order.
